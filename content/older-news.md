## 2020

- **June 15, 2020: Statement on racial injustice.** The lab publicly condemned police brutality against Black communities and the systemic racism behind it. The statement acknowledged that physics and medical research have long excluded underrepresented groups, and that institutions, including Northeastern and its Physics Department, should do more against anti-Black racism. The lab committed to pressing for concrete outreach and recruiting steps that diversify the physics community.
- **May 1, 2020: Eric Kercher defends his dissertation.** "New Methods and Tools for Optimizing Precision Photomedicine Research." Eric was the lab's first Ph.D. student and first graduate, with eight papers published or under review spanning photomedicine, cancer biology and device development.
- **April 1, 2020: NCI R01 award.** "Multiplexed and dynamically targeted photoimmunotherapy of heterogeneous, chemoresistant micrometastases guided by online in vivo optical imaging of cell-surface biomarkers" (R01 CA226855, $3.2M).

## 2019

- **June 28 – July 4, 2019: International Photodynamic Association World Congress (Boston).** The group presented several posters. Eric Kercher and Arvind Mohan each earned a Poster of Excellence (top 10) and an invited slam talk. Ryan Lang earned a Poster of Merit (top 20).
- **June 6, 2019: Eric Kercher passes his preliminary thesis exam.** The group celebrated over dinner at Petit Robert Bistro.
- **February 1–5, 2019: SPIE Photonics West (San Francisco).**
  - Eric Kercher, poster on real-time GPU-accelerated hyperspectral unmixing.
  - Ryan Lang, contributed talk on micro-image mosaicking for video-rate multichannel fluorescence microendoscopy.
  - Kai Zhang, contributed talk on custom fabrication of a high-performance femtosecond fiber laser for multiphoton microscopy.
  - Bryan Spring, invited talk on targeting drug-resistant glioblastoma stem cells with photodynamic therapy.

## 2018

- **September 6, 2018: Smith Family Foundation scientific poster session.** Kai Zhang, Eric Kercher, Ryan Lang and Taresh Sharan presented the lab's work. Prof. Spring received a Smith Family Award for Excellence in Biomedical Research in 2017 for "Peering into Cancer Stem Cell Niches to Guide Suppression of Multiple Signaling Loop Pathways." The program supports newly independent faculty with $300,000 over three years.
- **May 15, 2018: American Society for Photobiology conference (Tampa Bay).** Ryan Lang, Kai Zhang, Arvind Mohan and Prof. Spring gave presentations. During the same trip, Prof. Spring visited Moffitt Cancer Center with Jose Conejo-Garcia, Heiko Enderling and Bob Gatenby to explore a collaboration among mathematical oncologists, biomedical physicists and ovarian tumor immunologists.
